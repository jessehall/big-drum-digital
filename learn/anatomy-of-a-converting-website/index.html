Anatomy of a well-converting site

Make it easy for the right visitor to understand your business and take the next step.

Start with the visitor’s goal

Your visitor is trying to solve a problem. Put that problem and your offer near the top of the page. A pet groomer might say: “A clean, happy dog. Without the bath-time battle.” Follow it with what you do and where you do it.

Give the page a simple structure

Use a clear headline, a short explanation, a primary action, real customer proof, a three-step plan, and answers to common questions. Every section should help someone decide whether you can help them.

Make the next step obvious

Choose the action that fits your business: book a visit, request an estimate, or call. Repeat the same action where it is useful. Tell people what happens after they click. A phone link should work on mobile.

Show proof you can stand behind

Use genuine reviews with permission and accurate attribution. Show real work, explain your role, and give specific results only when you have records to support them. Do not invent numbers or testimonials.

Check the journey on your phone

Open the site on a small screen. Can you read the headline, find the contact button, complete the form, and see a clear confirmation? Measure inquiries alongside visits. A good-looking site still needs a working path to contact you.

Your one thing today

Open your homepage on your phone. Ask someone unfamiliar with your business what you do and what they should click. Rewrite the headline or button if their answer is unclear.
